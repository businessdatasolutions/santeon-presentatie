0 · How "mature" is rare — five instruments, one story

D3.js — bar chart

Cross-instrument comparison — threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison

Five independent surveys, five different definitions of "success," five numbers that all land in the same narrow band. Adoption breadth (78–88%) is high; maturity depth is not.

Hover a bar for the instrument's methodology note.

Nov 2026: the five numbers still stand. Every one of them measures the firm. In August the wiki added the individual worker as a separate unit of analysis (see section 12). The evidence is one self-reported case, and the axis is not yet validated. Adjacent figures since July measure value or production, not maturity, so they are not drawn as bars: about 30% of pilots reach production (one firm's index, BBC AI Decoded), and BCG finds that "just 26% of organizations have achieved value from AI". A caution on the adoption side: Goldman Sachs (Hatzius) attributes the widely cited jump in US firm adoption (~10% → >20%) to a looser survey question. Sources: threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison, concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.

12 · Worker layer — AI Worker Maturity, L0 → L5

Mermaid

ai-wiki synthesis, Aug 2026 (17 sources; confidence 0.80) — syntheses/ai-worker-maturity-levels

"How mature is the individual, not the firm?" Six levels, each defined by who produces, who checks and who decides, not by which tools someone owns. Beutler's ladder (section 9) maps onto L1–L3.

flowchart LR
  L0["L0 Bystander<br/>no meaningful AI use"] --> L1["L1 Conversationalist<br/>chat, drafts, summaries;<br/>output taken at face value"]
  L1 --> L2["L2 Operator<br/>iterates, adds context,<br/>verifies before use"]
  L2 --> L3["L3 Delegator<br/>hands over whole tasks<br/>against explicit specs"]
  L3 --> L4["L4 Orchestrator<br/>runs several agents;<br/>designs the environment"]
  L4 --> L5["L5 Multiplier<br/>builds the systems<br/>others work in"]
  L1 -.- A1(["≈ Ask"])
  L2 -.- A2(["≈ Assist"])
  L3 -.- A3(["≈ Automate (entry)"])
  style L0 fill:#f1f3f5,stroke:#868e96
  style L1 fill:#e7f5ff,stroke:#1971c2
  style L2 fill:#a5d8ff,stroke:#1971c2
  style L3 fill:#74c0fc,stroke:#1971c2
  style L4 fill:#1971c2,stroke:#0b4a86,color:#fff
  style L5 fill:#0b4a86,stroke:#0b4a86,color:#fff

Scored on six dimensions (tooling, delegation, verification, context design, scope of judgment, learning). A real profile is usually uneven across them. The fusion is wiki-authored and not validated against outcomes, and self-assessment is known to be biased, so score against behaviour, not identity.
