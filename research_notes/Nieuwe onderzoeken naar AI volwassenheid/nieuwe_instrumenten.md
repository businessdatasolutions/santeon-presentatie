# New 2025–2026 instruments measuring AI maturity / value at scale (excluding the five known instruments)

Scope: cross-industry studies published January 2025 – November 2026 that are NOT Stanford AI Index/McKinsey "Superagency" (1%), MIT CISR (7%), Werner-Le-Brun (12%), Cisco AI Readiness Index (13%) or MIT SMR–BCG (15%). Research date: 2026-11-07. "Breadth" = any use. "Depth" = maturity, scale, measurable value or P&L impact. All figures are self-reported by executives unless marked otherwise.

## 1. Which major studies appeared, and what depth figure does each report?

### Takeaway
At least a dozen new instruments appeared. The ones with a strict firm-level definition of "mature" or "value at scale" put the share at 5–13%: MIT NANDA 5%, BCG 5% (2025) rising to 7.5% (2026), Accenture 8%, McKinsey ~6%, PwC 12%, Gartner 13% (2026). Instruments that count initiatives or partial production put it at 16–25% (IBM, Deloitte, Gartner "1 in 5"). Instruments that ask "any positive ROI / measurable value?" report 60–97% (Wharton/GBK, KPMG, EY). These are definitional differences, not contradictions.

### Cited Findings

#### A. MIT Project NANDA — "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (July 2025, publicised Aug 2025)
- Publisher: Project NANDA at MIT (report marked "Preliminary Findings from AI Implementation Research from Project NANDA"; reviewer Pradyumna Chari; PDF metadata names Aditya Challapally as author; file created 13 July 2025). Research period "January – June 2025" — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Method: "a systematic review of over 300 publicly disclosed AI initiatives, structured interviews with representatives from 52 organizations, and survey responses from 153 senior leaders collected across four major industry conferences." No country/sector breakdown given — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Headline: "95% of organizations are getting zero return" despite "$30–40 billion in enterprise investment"; "Just 5% of integrated AI pilots are extracting millions in value, while the vast majority remain stuck with no measurable P&L impact." — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Exact success definition: "We define successfully implemented for task-specific GenAI tools as ones users or executives have remarked as causing a marked and sustained productivity and/or P&L impact." — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Funnel exhibit: general-purpose LLMs ~80% investigated, ~50% piloted, ~40% implemented; embedded/task-specific GenAI ~60% investigated, ~20% piloted, 5% successfully implemented. "only 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production." — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Breadth: ChatGPT/Copilot: "Over 80 percent of organizations have explored or piloted them, and nearly 40 percent report deployment." "Only 5% of enterprises have AI tools integrated in workflows at scale and 7 of 9 sectors show no real structural change." — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- "Shadow AI": "only 40% of companies say they purchased an official LLM subscription, workers from over 90% of the companies we surveyed reported regular use" of personal AI tools — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Self-stated limitation: "These figures are directionally accurate based on individual interviews rather than official company reporting. Sample sizes vary by category, and success definitions may differ across organizations." — [MIT NANDA report PDF](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)
- Critique: Wharton's Stefano Puntoni contrasts it with the Wharton/GBK study: "Our definition is very different from the MIT report. Theirs is much more stringent." — [Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/wait-are-74-of-businesses-actually)
- Critique: Wing VC calls the study problematic and says it got wide attention "despite widely recognized flaws"; Wing's own survey of 181 leaders found enterprises expect a median 50% of pilots to convert to production (search-snippet level; page not fetched) — [Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/the-state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise)
- Critique themes in commentary: small, conference-recruited sample (52 interviews behind the 95%); a six-month P&L window that ignores efficiency gains; non-peer-reviewed; framing that promotes NANDA's agent agenda (commentary, not formal review) — [PureAI](https://pureai.com/Articles/2025/09/23/Most-GenAI-Investments-Fail.aspx); [AI Realist (Sukhareva)](https://msukhareva.substack.com/p/genai-has-little-to-no-return-on)
- Quality: university-branded but preliminary, non-peer-reviewed, convenience sample, interview-derived self-report; numerator unit shifts between "organizations", "pilots" and "custom tools".

#### B. BCG — "The Widening AI Value Gap: Build for the Future 2025" (30 Sept 2025)
- Sample: "1,250 senior executives and AI decision makers", global, nine industries and 25+ sectors; maturity assessed across "41 foundational capabilities, covering strategy, tech, people, innovation, and outcomes". Fieldwork dates and country split not stated on the press page — [BCG press release 30 Sept 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings)
- Depth: 5% "future-built"; 35% "scalers" beginning to generate value; 60% "laggards" reporting "minimal revenue and cost gains" — [BCG press release 30 Sept 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings)
- Some secondary summaries split laggards into stagnating (14%) and emerging (46%) (not verified on BCG page) — [SoftwareSeni summary](https://www.softwareseni.com/the-widening-ai-value-gap-what-the-5-percent-do-that-the-60-percent-do-not/)
- Outcomes: future-built show 1.7x revenue growth, 3.6x three-year TSR, 1.6x EBIT margin vs laggards; agents = 17% of AI value in 2025, expected 29% by 2028 — [BCG press release 30 Sept 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings); full report [PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-October-2025.pdf)
- Quality: consultancy survey, self-reported capability scoring; classification threshold not published on the press page.

#### G. Deloitte AI Institute — "State of AI in the Enterprise: The Untapped Edge" (January 2026)
- Sample: 3,235 director-to-C-suite respondents, 24 countries, six industries; fieldwork Aug–Sep 2025 (secondary) — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise-2026-deloitte-maps-the-untapped-edge-of-enterprise-ai/); Deloitte NL says "over 3,000 executives worldwide with direct involvement in their companies' AI initiatives" — [Deloitte NL press release](https://www.deloitte.com/nl/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise-2026.html)

### Inferences
- Strict, firm-level "value at scale" instruments released in this window cluster at 5–13% (NANDA 5%, BCG 5% → 7.5%, McKinsey ~6%, Accenture 8%, PwC 12%, Gartner CIO 13% / vanguards 11%). This sits inside the 1–15% band of the five known instruments.
- The 60–97% "positive ROI" figures (Wharton/GBK, KPMG, EY) come from US large-firm samples, self-reported, with loose definitions (any productivity gain counts). They do not measure the same construct as "mature" or "P&L impact at scale".
- Numerator units differ: firms (BCG, Accenture, PwC, Gartner), initiatives (IBM 16%/25%, Gartner "1 in 5"), pilots/tools (NANDA 5%), share of pilots in production (Deloitte 25% of firms with ≥40% in production). Compare like with like.

### Gaps
- No primary full-text access for the PwC, IBM 2025, S&P Global, ServiceNow, Gartner 2026 and Accenture reports: sample splits and exact fieldwork dates are partly missing.
- ServiceNow 2025 sample size/countries not verified.
- Share of firms classified as "high maturity" in Gartner's June 2025 survey is not published in the release.
- Share of firms that are Microsoft "Frontier Firms" was not found.
- Attribution of the EY 97% figure to a 2025 wave is unverified.
- Stanford DEL "Canaries in the Coal Mine" (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025, payroll data) was not checked in this pass.
- No healthcare-specific cut was collected (out of scope).

## 2. European and Dutch official statistics (breadth only)

### Takeaway
Official statistics, which survey all firms with 10+ employees, show much lower breadth than consultancy panels of large firms: EU 20% (2025), Netherlands 33% (2025, CBS, preliminary), US 18–25% of firms (Census BTOS). The 78–88% "adoption" band applies only to large-firm samples.

- The Netherlands (33%) is well above the EU average (20%), and Dutch use grew 14% → 23% → 33% in two years. The CBS figure and the secondary Eurostat reading (33.21%) agree.

## 3. Do rigorous studies (academic, NBER, central banks, OECD) contradict "maturity is rare", or show it shifting upward?

### Takeaway
Rigorous studies do not contradict it. They find broad use in large firms but small measured effects: +0.2% annual labour-productivity growth (NBER, ~9,000 executives), precise null earnings effects (Denmark), and low daily intensity of use (Fed). The only upward shift is in consultancy surveys: BCG future-built 5% → 7.5% and scalers 35% → 41%; Deloitte "transformative" share doubled to 25%; KPMG agent building 53% → 62%. Gartner's October 2026 CIO survey (13%) shows that strict value remains rare.

### Cited Findings
- NBER WP 34836 "Firm Data on AI" (Yotzov, Barrero, Bloom, Bunn, Davis et al.; issued Feb 2026, revised Oct 2026): nearly 9,000 CFOs, CEOs and senior executives in the US, UK, Germany, Australia, Canada, Mexico and South Africa (fieldwork summer 2026); 78% of firms use AI; 80% of executives use it regularly (2.1 h/week, up from 1.5); 2% of firms account for over 50% of AI spending; essentially no net employment effect over 3 years; AI "raised annual labor productivity growth by 0.2%" over the past 3 years, with 0.6% per year expected next 3 years — [NBER w34836](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34836)
- Earlier version (Feb/Mar 2026, ~6,000 executives in US, UK, Germany, Australia): about nine in ten reported no impact on employment or productivity over the past three years (secondary) — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/90percent-of-firms-see-no-measurable-ai-impact-on-productivity-or-jobs-study-51625); [Atlanta Fed WP 2026-03](https://atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/working-papers/2026/03/24/03-firm-data-on-ai)
- Fed FEDS Note (Jeffrey S. Allen, 3 Apr 2026): firm adoption ranges from ~18% (BTOS, firm-weighted) to 69.4% (Atlanta Fed SBU, equal-weighted) and ~78% (SBU, employment-weighted); LLM use 46.4% / ~54%; 40.7% of workers use GenAI for their job (RPS, Nov 2025) but only 12% daily (Gallup mid-2025: 8% daily); 35% of SBU respondents use AI up to one hour a week — [Federal Reserve FEDS Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- Humlum & Vestergaard, "Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects" (NBER WP 33777, May 2025; Danish surveys linked to administrative records, 11 exposed occupations): "precise null effects on earnings and hours" two years after ChatGPT, confidence intervals ruling out effects above ~1–2%; average time savings ~3%; no firm-level effects on employment or wage bills — [BFI working paper PDF](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-3.pdf); [EconPapers NBER 33777](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:33777); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/05/18/ai-chatbots-study-impact-earnings-hours-worked-any-occupation/)
- OECD (2025): among SMEs using GenAI, only 29% use it in core business activities (secondary) — [Libertify summary of OECD](https://www.libertify.com/interactive-library/oecd-ai-adoption-smes/)
- Upward shift (consultancy): BCG 5% future-built + 35% scalers (2025) → 7.5% + 41% (2026) — [BCG 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings); [BCG 2026](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2026-ai-starting-to-pay-off-companies-generate-value)
- Upward shift (consultancy): Deloitte share reporting a transformative effect "more than double the previous year" at 25% — [Deloitte NL](https://www.deloitte.com/nl/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise-2026.html)
- Agentic upswing in activity, not yet in value: KPMG 62% building/deploying/developing agents (Q3 2026) — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/news/q3-ai-pulse-2026.html); McKinsey ≤10% scaling agents in any function (Nov 2025) — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/artificial-intelligence/the-state-of-ai-2025); BCG only 5% have full agent controls — [BCG 2026](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2026-ai-starting-to-pay-off-companies-generate-value); Deloitte 21% mature agent governance — [Deloitte NL](https://www.deloitte.com/nl/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise-2026.html); Gartner >40% of agentic projects forecast to be cancelled by 2027 — [The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/06/25/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2027-gartner-says)
- Counter-signal (downward): S&P Global 42% abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025 vs 17% in 2024 — [CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-project-fail-data-SPGlobal/742590/); ServiceNow average maturity fell 44 → 35 — [ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/workflow/ai/enterprise-ai-maturity-index-2025.html)

### Inferences
- The rigorous evidence supports the "broad but shallow" reading: use is widespread in large firms, but measured productivity effects are small (+0.2%/yr) and AI spending is highly concentrated (2% of firms = half of spend). Concentrated spend is consistent with a small "mature" tail.
- The 2026 upward shift is modest at the strict end (BCG +2.5 pp) and larger in the middle tier ("scaling"). The strict band moves from roughly 5–12% (2025 data) to 7.5–13% (2026 data). It does not leave the 1–15% range.
- Agentic AI raises activity (pilots, building) faster than governance or value; current evidence does not show agentic AI lifting the mature share above 15%.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study with observed (non-self-reported) firm-level P&L impact from AI at scale was found for 2025–2026.
- No ECB, Bank of England or DNB firm-survey figures on AI value were collected in this pass (the NBER paper includes UK DMP data, but no separate central-bank publication was checked).
- No 2026 MIT NANDA follow-up was found.

## 4. Is there a recognisable convergence band for 2025–2026, and how does it compare to 1–15%?

### Takeaway
Yes. Instruments with a strict definition (firm-level maturity, enterprise-wide scale, or significant/P&L value) converge on 5–13%, inside the known 1–15% band. A second, wider band of 16–25% appears for initiative-level or partial-production measures. Loose self-reported "positive ROI" measures sit at 60–97% and measure a different construct.

### Cited Findings
- Strict band (5–13%): MIT NANDA 5% of task-specific tools successfully implemented — [MIT NANDA](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf); BCG 5% (2025) — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings); McKinsey ~6% high performers — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/artificial-intelligence/the-state-of-ai-2025); BCG 7.5% (2026) — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2026-ai-starting-to-pay-off-companies-generate-value); Accenture 8% front-runners — [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/data-ai/front-runners-guide-scaling-ai); IBM 2026 10% of CEOs say AI drives growth (secondary) — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/seven-in-ten-ceos-say-ai-is-already-changing-their-core-business-but-only-10-say-its-driving-growth-529790-2026-05-05); PwC 12% both cost and revenue benefits — [Management Today](https://www.managementtoday.co.uk/half-ceos-havent-realised-financial-benefits-ai-integrations/indepth/article/1945663); Gartner 13% significant/transformational value (2026) — [Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/763088/gartner-slechts-13-procent-van-de-cios-ziet-grote-waarde-van-ai)
- Middle band (16–25%): IBM 16% of initiatives scaled enterprise-wide, 25% delivered expected ROI — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-ibm-study-ai-roi/); Gartner "one in five" AI applications succeed — [Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/763088/gartner-slechts-13-procent-van-de-cios-ziet-grote-waarde-van-ai); Deloitte 25% of firms moved ≥40% of pilots into production, 25% report transformative effect — [Deloitte NL](https://www.deloitte.com/nl/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise-2026.html); ServiceNow Pacesetters 18.2% (but top score only 57.9/100) — [ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/workflow/ai/enterprise-ai-maturity-index-2025.html)
- Loose band (≈60–97%): KPMG "nearly 6 in 10" measurable value — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/news/q3-ai-pulse-2026.html); Wharton/GBK 74% positive ROI among measurers — [Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/wait-are-74-of-businesses-actually); EY 97% positive ROI (Dec 2024 release) — [EY](https://ey.com/en_us/newsroom/2024/12/ey-research-artificial-intelligence-investments-set-to-remain-strong-in-2025-but-senior-leaders-recognize-emerging-risks)
- Mirror image: share reporting no material value — BCG 60% laggards (2025) — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings); PwC 56% no significant financial benefit — [tech.co](https://tech.co/news/ceos-havent-see-ai-revenue-cost-benefits); MIT NANDA "95% ... zero return" — [MIT NANDA](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf); NBER early version ~90% no productivity/employment impact — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/90percent-of-firms-see-no-measurable-ai-impact-on-productivity-or-jobs-study-51625)
- Breadth in the same large-firm surveys stays high: NBER 78% of firms use AI — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34836); Wharton 82% of leaders use GenAI weekly — [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/special-report/2025-ai-adoption-report); MIT NANDA >80% explored/piloted general-purpose LLMs — [MIT NANDA](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- The new instruments confirm the picture: breadth 78–88% in large-firm samples, depth 5–13% on strict definitions. The strict depth band of the new instruments (5–13%) is narrower than, and sits inside, the 1–15% band of the five known instruments.
- Movement in 2026 is small and upward at the strict end (BCG 5% → 7.5%; Gartner 13%; PwC 12%). It is larger in a "scaling" middle tier (BCG 41%), which explains headlines such as "almost 50% now generate value".
- Wherever a study reports a high success share, the definition is looser (any positive ROI, self-reported, US large firms that measure ROI). Presenting 74% or 97% next to 5% without the definition would mislead.
- Possible talking point: "Vrijwel elk groot bedrijf gebruikt AI; ongeveer één op de tien haalt er aantoonbaar waarde op schaal uit. Dat beeld is in 2026 nauwelijks veranderd." This is an inference from the sources above, not a quote.

### Gaps
- No study used a common definition across years and publishers, so the "band" is a comparison of non-identical constructs.
- Almost every depth figure is executive self-report; no observed, audited measure of AI value at firm level was found.
- Non-US, non-large-firm depth data (for example Dutch or EU firms, or hospitals) are missing from all instruments found.
