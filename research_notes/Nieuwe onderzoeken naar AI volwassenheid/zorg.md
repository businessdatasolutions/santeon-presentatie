# AI maturity, scaled adoption and value capture in hospitals and healthcare (Jan 2025 – Nov 2026)

Scope note: "breadth" = any use, pilots, at least one tool. "Depth" = scaled / enterprise-wide / full deployment / measurable value. Quality labels: **official statistics**, **academic** (peer-reviewed or government researchers), **consultancy/vendor survey** (commercial interest, often self-selected samples). All dates checked against the primary page unless marked "secondary".

## 1. Netherlands: 2025–2026 measurements of AI use or maturity in Dutch hospitals

### Takeaway
The best Dutch source is the M&I/Partners **AI Monitor Ziekenhuizen 2026** (8th edition, 52 hospitals = 73% of all Dutch hospitals, including all seven Santeon hospitals). Breadth is near-total (96% use AI beyond "very small scale", 90% use generative AI), but depth is thin: 8% of hospitals apply AI on a "large scale", 0% on a "very large scale", on average 1 application per hospital is "opgeschaald", and the realised impact over three years is "neutraal" on all five quintuple-aim goals. The RIVM Monitor Digitale Zorg 2025 (official) adds individual use: 53% of doctors and 41% of nurses use at least one AI application.

### Cited Findings

**M&I/Partners – AI Monitor Ziekenhuizen 2026 (8th edition)** — consultancy survey, but near-census of Dutch hospitals; anonymous, self-reported by mostly CIOs
- Publisher/date: M&I/Partners; press coverage dated 16 March 2026 ([Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/research/727294/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026-hoge-verwachtingen-van-ai-nog-geen-realiteit)) and 14 April 2026 ([Consultancy.nl](https://www.consultancy.nl/nieuws/66872/ziekenhuizen-zetten-stappen-met-ai-maar-maken-de-hoge-verwachtingen-nog-niet-waar)). Primary PDF: [mxi.nl – AI Monitor Ziekenhuizen 2026](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- Sample: "een recordaantal van 52 ziekenhuizen (73% van het totaal)"; 22 general, 26 topklinisch, 4 academic; 56 respondents, of whom 18 CIOs, 8 AI programme managers, 8 clinical informaticians, 3 CMIOs, 3 CDO/data managers, 2 clinical physicists. The introduction states fieldwork "januari tot en met februari 2025" — almost certainly a typo for 2026, since this is the 2026 edition (the 7th edition was published April 2025) — [M&I PDF p. 3, 11](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **Santeon relevance:** the participant list (p. 10) includes Canisius Wilhelmina Ziekenhuis, Catharina Ziekenhuis, Maasstad Ziekenhuis, Martini Ziekenhuis, Medisch Spectrum Twente, OLVG and St. Antonius Ziekenhuis, i.e. all seven Santeon hospitals. Results are not broken down per hospital — [M&I PDF](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **Depth – scale question** ("Op welke schaal wordt AI binnen uw ziekenhuis toegepast?", n=52, single choice, five levels). Chart values 2026 (2025): zeer kleine schaal/niet 4% (13%); kleine schaal 42% (53%); gemiddeld 46% (33%); grote schaal 8% (3%); zeer grote schaal 0% (0%) — [M&I PDF p. 25](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
  - **Discrepancy to flag:** the report's own conclusions text and all press coverage say "Bij 53% blijft AI nog kleinschalig" and "kleinschalige inzet substantieel (53%; -11%)". The chart (verified visually) shows 53% is the **2025** value and **42%** is 2026 (53 − 11 = 42; 4 + 42 + 46 + 8 = 100). The text's "+23%" for medium scale also does not match the chart (33% → 46% = +13). Use the chart values; "8% grootschalig, 0% zeer grootschalig" is consistent in all sources — [M&I PDF p. 6, 25](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf); [Consultancy.nl](https://www.consultancy.nl/nieuws/66872/ziekenhuizen-zetten-stappen-met-ai-maar-maken-de-hoge-verwachtingen-nog-niet-waar)
  - The scale levels are not defined in the questionnaire excerpt; they are the respondent's self-assessment.
- **Depth – innovation funnel** (n=51): on average 18 AI applications per hospital: 8 idea/exploration, 3 purchase/development, 3 pilot/validation, **1 implementation, 1 scaled up ("opgeschaald in de praktijk")**, 2 in beheer. Top 10% hospitals: 23 / 9 / 7 / 4 / 2 / 8 — [M&I PDF p. 26](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **Depth – realised value** (n=51, new question, "gerealiseerde impact … afgelopen drie jaar"): 55% report a positive impact on employee experience; "In 47% van de gevallen heeft AI geleid tot hogere zorgkosten"; "In de meeste ziekenhuizen heeft AI op alle vijf de aims in de zorg een neutrale impact gehad"; for every aim some hospitals report a very negative impact. Expected impact over five years: positive for employees 89%, patients 81%, health outcomes 49%; costs: 46% expect negative, only 14% positive (−27 pts) — [M&I PDF p. 36–37](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **Depth – ambient scribes** (spraakgestuurd rapporteren): 69% have started; 42% pilot, 10% implementing, 17% scaling, 0% beheer; "gemiddeld gebruikt 21% van de beoogde zorgverleners binnen ziekenhuizen deze technologie actief"; only 4 hospitals (12%) reach >70% of the intended users. Market leader Autoscriber (38%) — [M&I PDF p. 31](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **AI agents:** 38% not active, 48% idea/exploration, 2% purchase, 10% pilot, 4% implementation, 0% scaling, 2% beheer — [M&I PDF p. 32](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **Breadth:** generative AI used by 90% of hospitals (+15 pts vs 2025, +33 vs 2024); traditional AI 83% (stable). Of all AI applications 53% traditional vs 47% generative. Domains: administration and billing 80% (+32), diagnostics 71%, communication/chatbots 65%, research 53%, logistics 49%, coding 31%, overdracht/ontslag 22%, prognosis 18%. Radiology still the main specialty (41%, down 21 pts) — [M&I PDF p. 27–28, 30](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- **Governance/readiness:** 48% have an AI vision and strategy (+20); 76% have AI policy; 60% have an AI board; 53% already use high-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act, 16% have not done the risk classification. Readiness "grotendeels of volledig op orde": AI team 51%, vision/strategy 45%; not in order: financial room 45%, staff AI awareness 42%, transformation capacity 39%. 25% have no readiness component in order; none has all seven — [M&I PDF p. 6–7, 33, 38](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- Drivers: reducing workload 100%, improving quality of care 88% — [M&I PDF p. 35](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)

**M&I/Partners – AI Monitor Ziekenhuizen 2025 (7th edition), April 2025** — baseline for the trend (same consultancy survey)
- 4 April 2025; Skipr reports 47 hospitals surveyed; other coverage says 49 representatives of 46 hospitals. 75% use generative AI. Only one hospital applies AI large-scale. Average per hospital: 3 applications in development, 4 in pilot, 2 implemented, 5 in production of which 2 judged valuable from practice. 30% of hospitals: unclear who is ultimately responsible for AI. Domains: diagnostic support 68% (radiology 62%), care administration 48% — [Skipr](https://www.skipr.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-nog-nauwelijks-ai-implementatie-in-ziekenhuizen-op-grote-schaal/); [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/2025/04/04/ziekenhuizen-nog-niet-klaar-voor-grootschalige-adoptie-van-ai/)
- Secondary sources disagree on the 2025 scale split (Skipr: "een kwart" medium; Computable: "een derde"). The 2026 report's chart gives the 2025 values as 13 / 53 / 33 / 3 / 0 — use those — [M&I PDF p. 25](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf)
- Warning: aggregator sites such as theaidaily.nl present "93% of hospitals use AI" as a 2025 figure; a search summary indicated it refers to an older edition (not verified). Do not use aggregator figures; cite the M&I PDF — [theaidaily.nl](https://theaidaily.nl/statistieken/ai-zorg-statistieken-2026/)

**RIVM – Monitor Digitale Zorg 2025 (RIVM-rapport 2025-0176)** — official statistics (government institute, Nivel panels, representative)
- Published 9 March 2026; data collection April–July 2025 via four Nivel panels (e.g. Nivel Consumentenpanel, Nivel Panel Verpleging & Verzorging) — [RIVM publication page](https://www.rivm.nl/publicaties/monitor-digitale-zorg-2025-stand-van-zaken-van-digitale-zorg-in-nederland); [RIVM report PDF](https://www.rivm.nl/bibliotheek/rapporten/2025-0176.pdf)
- "In 2024 gaf 13% van de artsen aan een of meerdere AI-toepassingen te gebruiken. Dat percentage is in 2025 fors gestegen naar 53%." RIVM itself warns that answer options changed, so "geen goede vergelijking tussen beide jaren" is possible. Nurses (asked first in 2025): 41% use AI. Main uses: looking up information (guidelines, literature), administration (speech-driven reporting, capacity planning, billing), education — [RIVM report p. 16](https://www.rivm.nl/bibliotheek/rapporten/2025-0176.pdf)
- This is breadth only ("one or more AI applications"); there is no depth measure.

**Philips – Future Health Index 2025, Netherlands edition** — vendor survey (Accenture Song for Philips)
- NL sample: 102 healthcare professionals and 977 patients, December 2024 – March 2025 (secondary: search summary of Dutch coverage). Separate Philips leadership study: 2,800 executives in 14 countries, 200 in NL; three-quarters think professionals remain sceptical about automation — [Dutch IT Channel, 16 June 2025](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/647536/ai-kan-werknemers-in-zorg-verbeteren-maar-vertrouwen-is-drempel). No deployment-at-scale figure.

**Deloitte – 2026 Global Health Care Outlook** (includes NL respondents; see section 3 for figures) — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/industry/health-care/life-sciences-and-health-care-industry-outlooks/2026-global-health-care-outlook.html)

**NVZ/NFU position paper "AI in de zorg: versneld en verantwoord opschalen"** (September 2024, written by M&I/Partners) — policy paper, baseline, no hospital adoption figures found — [NFU summary PDF](https://www.nfu.nl/sites/default/files/2024-09/NVZ_NFU_Position_Paper_AI-in-de-zorg-Samenvatting-september-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- For this audience the M&I 2026 monitor is the strongest "own sector" counterpart: the Santeon hospitals took part, so the speaker can say "jullie eigen ziekenhuizen zitten in deze cijfers". A clean slide pair: breadth 90% (generative AI) / 96% (more than very small scale) vs depth 8% large scale, 0% very large scale, ~1 scaled application per hospital, realised impact "neutraal".
- Use 42% (not 53%) for "kleinschalig" in 2026, or avoid that number; 8% large-scale is safe.
- Ambient scribes are the best concrete depth example: 69% started, 21% of intended clinicians actually use it.

### Gaps
- No Nictiz "AI-monitor" or eHealth-monitor 2025 with AI figures was found; RIVM's Monitor Digitale Zorg is the successor-type official source.
- No CBS figure specific to hospitals (CBS publishes AI use by enterprises per sector, not checked here).
- No VWS, Zorginstituut, NL AIC or Santeon/Data Science Center publication with 2025–2026 adoption numbers was found in the time available.
- The M&I questionnaire does not define "grote schaal"; it is self-assessment.

## 2. Europe: EU, WHO Europe, OECD, HIMSS Europe, Philips

### Takeaway
European measurements are either country-level (WHO Europe: only 8% of 50 states have a health-specific AI strategy) or small, self-selected surveys (EU Commission study: 35 hospital representatives). None gives a robust "share of European hospitals with AI at scale". The EU study itself warns that surveys overestimate deployment.

### Cited Findings

**European Commission (DG SANTE) – Study on the deployment of AI in healthcare, Final report** — commissioned study (PwC EU Services and Open Evidence), January 2024 – May 2025, manuscript completed June 2025, published August 2025; doi 10.2875/2169577 — [Final report PDF (mirror)](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Final-Report.pdf); [Carnall Farrar summary](https://www.carnallfarrar.com/european-commission-study-on-the-deployment-of-ai-in-healthcare-across-the-eu/)
- Hospital representatives (n=35): 57% "currently piloting an AI solution", 54% "had already purchased and deployed a commercially available solution", 31% "developed and deployed an in-house AI solution"; only two had not adopted AI — [Final report p. 41–42](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Final-Report.pdf)
- Healthcare professionals (n=51, only those with "good knowledge of AI usage", from "technologically advanced hospitals"): 63% used or use AI tools; but "only 20 out of the 46 respondents (43%) provided evidence on the actual use of AI/ML-enabled medical devices", and four of the "users" named ChatGPT — [Final report p. 41](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Final-Report.pdf)
- The report's own caveat: "surveys may lead to overly optimistic estimations of AI deployment in healthcare"; respondents "may conflate traditional rule-based … systems … with more recent deep learning-based AI, which only remains in the early stages of deployment" — [Final report p. 41](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Final-Report.pdf)
- Baselines cited in the report: European Society of Radiology survey: 48% of 572 radiologists use AI in clinical practice; US job-posting study: fewer than 5% of healthcare organisations adopted AI, under 3% of hospitals posted AI jobs (older data) — [Final report p. 40–41](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Final-Report.pdf)
- A secondary claim circulating online ("94% of 300 providers use or plan AI") does **not** appear in the report text; 94% in the report is the share of 32 hospital representatives who see radiology as the most transformative area — [Final report p. 9](https://medicaldevices.ipn.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Final-Report.pdf). Do not use the "94% of 300" figure.

**WHO Regional Office for Europe – "Artificial intelligence is reshaping health systems: state of readiness across the WHO European Region"** — intergovernmental, country-level survey; 19 November 2025; 50 of 53 Member States; survey June 2024 – March 2025
- 4 of 50 (8%) have a published health-specific AI strategy, 7 (14%) are developing one; 66% have cross-sector AI strategies that include health; 4 (8%) have liability standards for AI in health; legal uncertainty is the top barrier for 86% (43 of 50), financial constraints 78% — [WHO publication](https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/WHO-EURO-2025-12707-52481-81028); [WHO statement](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/19-11-2025-statement---humanity-must-hold-the-pen--the-european-region-can-write-the-story-of-ethical-ai-for-health); [ICT&health international](https://www.icthealth.org/news/only-8-of-who-europe-states-have-health-ai-strategies); [WHO BIH report](https://bosniaherzegovina.un.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/WHO_BIH_Report_2025Issue15.pdf)
- No hospital-level adoption percentage.

**OECD – "Scaling artificial intelligence in health" (2026)** — intergovernmental, country-level (secondary only)
- All surveyed OECD countries use AI for administrative automation, 95% for imaging diagnostics, only 55% for predictive analytics/risk stratification/personalised treatment; fewer than one third of countries have acted to strengthen foundations or human capacity; seven countries have a specific AI-and-health strategy — secondary: [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/health/3854477-ai-in-health-systems-big-potential-slow-adoption-and-the-road-to-scale). These are country-level "any use" figures, not hospital maturity.

**Philips – Future Health Index 2025 (global)** — vendor survey; 15 May 2025; nearly 2,000 healthcare professionals and >16,000 patients in 16 countries (incl. NL)
- Over 75% of professionals lose clinical time to incomplete or inaccessible data; one third lose more than 45 minutes per shift; 69% of clinicians are involved in AI/digital development but only 38% think these tools meet real-world needs; over 75% unclear about liability for AI-driven errors. No deployment-at-scale figure — [Philips press release](https://www.philips.com/a-w/about/news/archive/standard/news/press/2025/philips-future-health-index-2025-ai-poised-to-transform-global-healthcare-urging-leaders-to-act-now.html)

**Other European signals (secondary, lower quality)**
- Corti/YouGov, February 2025: 1,794 healthcare professionals in UK, Denmark, France, Germany; on average 65% never use AI in their work (UK 73%) — secondary only, via search summary of [DJS Research](https://www.djsresearch.co.uk/HealthMarketResearchInsightsAndFindings/article/New-survey-suggests-the-UK-could-be-at-risk-of-falling-behind-in-the-adoption-of-AI-in-the-healthcare-industry-05663); not verified against the primary release.
- Black Book Research, June 2025: 442 executives in 13 European countries, focus on workforce; UK 78% "actively evaluating" AI clinical decision support — vendor-adjacent survey — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/european-hospitals-turn-to-technology-amid-intensifying-workforce-22586869)

### Inferences
- Europe has no equivalent of the US AHA/ASTP hospital statistics. The Dutch M&I monitor is, in practice, more robust than anything at EU level for hospitals.
- The EU study's own warning (surveys overestimate; only 43% could show actual use) is a useful quote for a sceptical audience.

### Gaps
- No HIMSS Europe 2025/2026 survey with a European hospital adoption or AMAM maturity distribution was found.
- EIT Health 2025–2026: not searched in depth; no figures found.
- OECD 2026 report not read in the primary; figures above are secondary.

## 3. International: US and multi-country surveys (AMA, ASTP/AHA, Menlo, Bain/KLAS, Deloitte, Qventus, Bessemer, Stanford)

### Takeaway
The international data show the same split as the cross-industry slide. Breadth: 81% of US physicians use AI (AMA 2026) and 71% of US hospitals use EHR-integrated predictive AI (official 2024 data). Depth: 2% of large health systems have deployed AI across the enterprise (Deloitte, six countries including NL), 4% of US health systems have "scaled AI with measurable outcomes" (Qventus 2026), only 30% of proofs of concept reach production (Bessemer/AWS/Bain 2025), and 51% have not measured returns (Deloitte).

### Cited Findings

**Official statistics / academic (US)**
- **ASTP/ONC Data Brief No. 80**, September 2025, "Hospital Trends in the Use, Evaluation, and Governance of Predictive AI, 2023–2024", based on the AHA Annual Survey IT Supplement (nationally representative non-federal acute care hospitals): 71% of hospitals used predictive AI integrated into their EHR in 2024 (66% in 2023); 82% evaluated for accuracy, 74% for bias, 79% did post-implementation monitoring; "don't know" 15% / 21% / 18%. Small, rural, independent and critical access hospitals lag — [ASTP data brief page](https://www.healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/hospital-trends-use-evaluation-and-governance-predictive-ai-2023-2024); [PDF](https://healthit.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Data-Brief-80-Hospital-Trends-in-the-Use-Evaluation-and-Governance-of-Predictive-AI-2023-2024_508.pdf)
- **Everson, Nong, Richwine – "Uptake of Generative AI Integrated With Electronic Health Records in US Hospitals"**, JAMA Network Open, 12 December 2025 (authors from HHS ASTP): 2,174 hospitals (51.5% response, weighted). 31.5% "early adopters" currently using an LLM integrated with the EHR in 2024; 24.7% plan within a year; 32.0% no plans. Major teaching 53.9% vs non-teaching 25.9%; system-affiliated 38.5% vs independent 16.3%; nonprofit 41.1% vs for-profit 6.5%. Use cases not captured — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12701511/)
- **Poon, Lemak, Rojas, Guptill, Classen – "Adoption of artificial intelligence in healthcare: survey of health system priorities, successes, and challenges"**, JAMIA, 5 May 2025: 43 US health systems (Scottsdale Institute), fall 2024, mostly CMIO/CIO-level. Shows the breadth–depth gap per use case ("any deployment" vs "full deployment"): ambient notes 60% / 14% (100% have some activity); imaging 90% / 40%; early sepsis detection 67% / 48%; clinical deterioration 56% / 37%; in-basket automation 51% / 14%; **risk of patient falls 36% / 19%**; data analysis 35% / 5%. "High success" reported for clinical documentation 53%, risk stratification 38%, revenue cycle 23%, diagnosis 19%. Top barrier: immature AI tools 77%. **Patient safety/quality is a top-two goal for 56%** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12202002/)
- **AMA – 2026 Physician Survey on Augmented Intelligence**, released March 2026: 1,692 US physicians, fielded 15 Jan – 2 Feb 2026. 81% use AI in their practice (38% in 2023); average 2.3 use cases (1.1 in 2023); top uses: summarising research/standards of care 39%, discharge instructions/care plans/progress notes 30%; 88% worry about skill loss; 92% want more training — professional-association survey — [AMA](https://ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/more-80-physicians-use-ai-professionally-ama-survey); [AMA press release](https://ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-ai-usage-among-doctors-doubles-confidence-technology-grows)
- **Stanford HAI – AI Index 2026**, new Chapter 6 "AI in Medicine" (with Schmidt Sciences), April 2026: ambient AI scribes saw substantial 2025 adoption, with physicians reporting up to 83% less time on notes; of 500+ clinical AI studies reviewed, nearly half rely on exam-style questions and only 5% use real clinical data — "rigorous evidence remains limited" — [Stanford HAI AI Index 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report) (figures taken from the user's ai-wiki summary of the report, `ai-wiki/wiki/sources/2026-04-30-ai-index-report-2026.md`; the Medicine chapter itself was not read)

**Consultancy / vendor / VC surveys (multi-country or US)**
- **Deloitte – 2026 Global Health Care Outlook**, 11 December 2025: 180 C-suite executives of large health systems in Australia, Canada, Germany, **the Netherlands**, UK and US, August–September 2025. "about 30% of surveyed health systems report operating gen AI at scale in select areas"; "**just 2% have deployed AI across their entire enterprise**"; "51% said they either haven't measured returns or determined that it is too soon to see results"; 31% moderate financial returns, **3% "significant" ROI**; only 22% of non-US respondents expect AI to be a major focus in 2026 (US 37%) — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/industry/health-care/life-sciences-and-health-care-industry-outlooks/2026-global-health-care-outlook.html). - **Qventus – second annual CIO report**, 9 April 2026: survey and interviews with 60+ US CIOs, chief AI officers, CMIOs. "only 4% of organizations have reached scaled AI with measurable outcomes"; 42% actively deploying across multiple use cases (secondary summary); 74% cite EHR dependency, 45% difficulty scaling pilots — vendor survey — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260409296812/en/Qventus-Releases-Second-Annual-CIO-Report-Revealing-How-Health-Systems-Are-Closing-the-Gap-Between-AI-Pilots-and-Payoff); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/only-4-of-health-systems-achieve-scaled-ai-roi-report/). Qventus 2025 report (12 June 2025): only 2% of IT leaders rated their EHR vendor's AI as "mature" (secondary) — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250612656244/en)
- **Bessemer Venture Partners, AWS, Bain – The Healthcare AI Adoption Index**, spring 2025: 400+ US payers, providers, pharma. "only 30% of completed POCs have made it into production"; providers ~35% (Bessemer page: large providers 46%), payers 31%, pharma 24%; 45% of use cases still in ideation/POC — VC/consultancy survey — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-healthcare-ai-adoption-index); [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/the-healthcare-ai-adoption-index/)
- **Menlo Ventures – "2025: The State of AI in Healthcare"**, 21 October 2025: 700+ US executives (410+ provider technology decision-makers), Morning Consult, Aug–Sep 2025. 22% of organisations "have implemented domain-specific AI tools" vs 9% of all US firms (Census BTOS, Sept 2025) → "2.2x faster" (22/9 is actually 2.4x); health systems 27%, outpatient 18%, payers 14%. "Adoption" is not formally defined. Menlo discusses its own portfolio companies — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-ai-in-healthcare/); [PDF](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/menlo_ventures_healthcare_ai_report-2025.pdf)
- **Bain & KLAS – Healthcare IT spending study**, 9 October 2025: ~228 US provider and payer executives; ambient documentation: about one in five providers at full rollout, two in five in pilot; 70% of providers have an AI strategy in place or in development (60% a year earlier) — [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/20252/healthcare-ai-investment-focusing-on-hard-dollar-returns-and-clinical-workflows-amid-shift-from-pilots-to-widespread-productionbain--company-and-klas-research/) (figures via search summary of the release)
- **Guidehouse & HIMSS – 2026 Healthcare AI Trends**: 50 senior leaders; 78% of health systems engaged in AI projects, only 52% have the operational capacity to implement successfully — secondary only, small sample — [Briefglance](https://briefglance.com/articles/healthcares-ai-paradox-high-investment-low-operational-readiness)

### Inferences
- The best "depth" numbers for a slide: Deloitte 2% enterprise-wide (includes Dutch health systems), Qventus 4% scaled with measurable outcomes, M&I 8% large-scale in NL. All three are in or below the cross-industry 1–15% band.
- The best "breadth" numbers: AMA 81% of physicians, M&I 90% generative AI in Dutch hospitals, ASTP 71% predictive AI in US hospitals. These sit at or above the cross-industry 78–88% band.
- Poon et al. is the most useful "same use case, two depths" table (e.g. ambient notes 60% any vs 14% full).

### Gaps
- KLAS stand-alone 2025 reports: a figure of 22% of organisations using generative AI (3,370 respondents, 1,742 organisations, December 2025) appeared only in a search summary; not verified.
- HIMSS AMAM (Analytics Maturity Adoption Model) stage distributions are not public; no 2025–2026 numbers found.
- Peterson Health Technology Institute (PHTI) 2025 reports evaluate ambient scribes' evidence but no adoption-maturity percentage was retrieved.
- AHA's own 2025–2026 reports beyond the ASTP brief were not checked.

## 4. AI in patient safety, quality management and incident reporting (VIM)

### Takeaway
No 2025–2026 survey measures how many hospitals use AI to classify or analyse incident reports. The evidence is research-stage (single-institution or two-institution studies) plus national ambitions (NHS England LFPSE). In the Dutch M&I monitor, quality, patient safety and incident reporting do not appear as a domain at all, although "verbeteren kwaliteit van zorg" is a driver for 88% of hospitals.

### Cited Findings

- For this audience, AI in incident reporting is not "behind" the rest of the hospital; it is barely on the map in any survey. That supports the session's message: start small at the Ask level, with a human deciding.

## 5. Is the healthcare "maturity" figure lower or higher than the cross-industry 1–15% band?

### Takeaway
Healthcare depth figures from 2025–2026 fall at the low end of or inside the 1–15% band (2% enterprise-wide, 3% significant ROI, 4% scaled with measurable outcomes, 8% large-scale in Dutch hospitals, 0% very large scale). Breadth figures are as high as or higher than the 78–88% band (81% of physicians, 90% of Dutch hospitals use generative AI, 96% beyond very small scale). Healthcare shows the same gap as other industries, and the Dutch figure is in the middle of it.

### Cited Findings
- Depth, healthcare: Deloitte 2% enterprise-wide and 3% significant ROI (180 health systems, six countries incl. NL) — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/industry/health-care/life-sciences-and-health-care-industry-outlooks/2026-global-health-care-outlook.html); Qventus 4% scaled with measurable outcomes (US) — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260409296812/en/Qventus-Releases-Second-Annual-CIO-Report-Revealing-How-Health-Systems-Are-Closing-the-Gap-Between-AI-Pilots-and-Payoff); M&I 8% large scale, 0% very large scale (NL) — [M&I PDF p. 25](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf); ambient-scribe use by 21% of intended clinicians (NL) — [M&I PDF p. 31](https://mxi.nl/uploads/files/publication/ai-monitor-ziekenhuizen-2026.pdf); 30% of POCs reach production — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-healthcare-ai-adoption-index)

### Inferences
- A healthcare slide can mirror the five cross-industry numbers one for one: "breed" (81–96%) vs "diep" (2–8%). The Dutch M&I figure (8% grootschalig, 0% zeer grootschalig) is the most relevant for this room, because their own hospitals are in the sample.
- Definitions differ per study (enterprise-wide, scaled with measurable outcomes, self-assessed "grote schaal"), so present them as a range, not as one comparable metric. This is the same caveat as for the cross-industry 1–15% band.

### Gaps
- No study measures healthcare and other industries with one identical maturity definition in 2025–2026; the comparison stays indirect.
- No Europe-wide hospital maturity distribution (e.g. HIMSS AMAM stages) was found.
