## 1. AI Index / McKinsey (slide: 1%): what does the AI Index 2026 report, and does McKinsey have newer maturity, high-performer, scaling or EBIT figures?

### Takeaway
The AI Index 2026 (April 2026) updates adoption breadth to 88% but does **not** repeat or update the "1% mature" figure; its closest depth measure is the McKinsey deployment-stage chart (only 3–10% "fully scaled", by company size). McKinsey's own surveys since then report a flat **~6% "AI high performers"** in both the Nov 2025 and Aug 2026 editions and **37–39%** reporting any EBIT impact. These measure value, not self-reported "maturity", so they are not a like-for-like replacement for the 1%.

### Cited Findings
**Baseline used on the slide**
- AI Index 2025 (Stanford HAI, April 2025): 78% of organisations use AI in at least one business function (55% in 2023); 71% use GenAI; "only 1% of C-suite executives describe their GenAI rollouts as 'mature'" — [Stanford HAI AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report) (as summarised in wiki `2026-04-28-ai-index-report-2025.md`)
- The 1% originates in McKinsey *Superagency in the workplace* (January 2025): "almost all companies invest in AI, but just 1 percent believe they are at maturity", with maturity defined as AI being fully integrated into workflows and driving substantial business outcomes — [McKinsey Superagency PDF](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/superagency%20in%20the%20workplace%20empowering%20people%20to%20unlock%20ais%20full%20potential%20at%20work/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential-v4.pdf) (definition taken from a search extract of this PDF; direct fetch not done). The coordinator gives the sample as n≈3,600 employees + 238 C-suite.

**Newer edition 1: Stanford HAI AI Index 2026 (April 2026)**
- Organisational AI use in at least one business function: **88% in 2025, up from 78% in 2024**; over half report three or more functions; GenAI regular use **79% (2025) vs 71% (2024)**. Source line on every chart: "McKinsey & Company Survey, 2025". The chapter warns the results "are self-reported and should be viewed as directional rather than comprehensive" — [AI Index 2026, Chapter 4 Economy (PDF), §4.3 Corporate AI Adoption](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_4_economy.pdf)
- Deployment stage by revenue (McKinsey 2025 survey, Figure 4.3.6): "Fully scaled" = **5%** (<$100M), **4%** ($100–499M), **3%** ($500–999M), **9%** ($1–4.9B), **10%** ($5B+). "Scaling" = 25% / 23% / 29% / 32% / 39% — [AI Index 2026, Ch. 4](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_4_economy.pdf)
- AI agents: "Scaled use was in the single digits for nearly all functions"; even in IT and knowledge management "about two-thirds or more of respondents reported no use" — [AI Index 2026, Ch. 4](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_4_economy.pdf)
- A text search of the extracted Economy chapter found no use of "mature"/"maturity" and no repeat of the 1% C-suite figure (own check of the PDF text) — [AI Index 2026, Ch. 4](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_4_economy.pdf)
- Separate measure: the AI Index and McKinsey ran a joint *responsible-AI* maturity survey for a second year; RAI maturity "improved across all regions from 2024 to 2025" but remains early-stage (search summary of hai.stanford.edu; not fetched) — [AI Index 2026 Economy page](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy). This is RAI governance maturity, not deployment maturity.

**Newer edition 2: McKinsey, "The state of AI: How organizations are rewiring to capture value" (March 2025)**
- Exists (authors Alex Singla, Alexander Sukharevsky, Lareina Yee). It is the survey behind the 78% in AI Index 2025 — [McKinsey PDF, March 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/2025/the-state-of-ai-how-organizations-are-rewiring-to-capture-value_final.pdf). Detailed figures were not retrieved (see Gaps).

**Newer edition 3: McKinsey, "The state of AI in 2025: Agents, innovation, and transformation" (5 November 2025)**
- Online survey, **1,993 participants** "at all levels of the organization", fieldwork **25 June – 29 July 2025** — [McKinsey report page](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025); [PDF](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/november%202025/the-state-of-ai-in-2025.pdf)
- 88% use AI in at least one function; "nearly two-thirds of respondents say their organizations have not yet begun scaling AI across the enterprise" — same sources
- **AI high performers**: respondents who attribute **≥5% of EBIT to AI** and report "significant" value = **about 6%** of respondents; they are nearly 3× as likely to have fundamentally redesigned workflows — same sources
- **39%** report any EBIT impact at enterprise level; most of those attribute <5% of EBIT to AI — same sources; corroborated by [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/mckinsey-report-shows-ai-interest-but-slow-scaling)
- Agents: **62%** at least experimenting; **23%** scaling an agentic system somewhere; in any single function **≤10%** scaling agents — same McKinsey sources
- Scale effect: nearly half of respondents at companies with >$5B revenue have reached the scaling phase vs 29% at <$100M — same McKinsey sources
- Note: the figures above come from search-engine extracts of the mckinsey.com page and PDF; direct WebFetch of mckinsey.com timed out repeatedly.

**Newer edition 4: McKinsey, "The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI" (August 2026; secondary sources give 25 August 2026)**
- Online survey **4 May – 8 June 2026**, **1,719 participants in 97 nations** — [McKinsey State of AI page](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) (search extract); [McKinsey PDF, Aug 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/the-state-of-ai-in-2026-on-the-road-to-roi-aug-2026.pdf); publication date 25 Aug 2026 per [explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/mckinsey-state-of-ai-2026-roi-agentic-coding-august-2026) (secondary)
- **AI high performers** (same definition: ≥5% of EBIT from AI and "significant" impact) = **6%**, "unchanged from 2025" — McKinsey page (search extract), corroborated by [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/mckinsey-state-of-ai-2026-enterprise-roi-gap/) (secondary)
- **37%** attribute some positive EBIT impact to AI, "roughly flat" vs last year — McKinsey page (search extract)
- Nearly three-quarters of high performers fundamentally redesigned workflows, up from 55% last year — McKinsey page (search extract)
- **80%** say AI improved their individual productivity; 50% say it helps them decide better — McKinsey page (search extract)
- Agents: **40%** of respondents at organisations with >$1B revenue report scaling AI agents, up from 27% — McKinsey page (search extract)
- ~20% say AI operating costs (incl. token costs) constrained their AI use — McKinsey page (search extract)
- Adoption **89%** (≥1 function) and **44%** "at least scaling AI across the enterprise" — only from secondary summaries ([adin.ai summary](https://www.adin.ai/marketing-radar/reports/library/the-state-of-ai-in-2026-on-the-road-to-roi/)); the same summary gives n=1,993 overall and n=1,719 for these figures, which conflicts with McKinsey's stated 1,719. Treat 89% and 44% as unverified.

**Related McKinsey 2026 item that repeats a "1% mature" figure**
- *The State of Organizations 2026*: "In the United States alone, only 1 percent of C-suite respondents describe their generative AI experimentation and rollouts as mature, and only 19 percent report clear strategies and action"; survey June–September 2025, n=10,018 (search extract of the McKinsey PDF) — [McKinsey, The State of Organizations 2026 (PDF)](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/people%20and%20organizational%20performance/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20organizations/2026/the-state-of-organizations-2026.pdf). The extract does not show whether this 1% is a new measurement from the n=10,018 survey or a re-citation of the *Superagency* figure.

### Inferences
- There is no newer, like-for-like successor to the "1% mature" number. The AI Index dropped it in 2026, and McKinsey's flagship survey uses "AI high performers" (value-based, ~6%) instead of self-reported maturity.
- If the slide wants an up-to-date McKinsey figure with a stable definition across two waves, "6% AI high performers (2025 and 2026, unchanged)" is the strongest candidate. It is a value threshold (≥5% of EBIT) rather than a maturity self-label, so the slide should label it as such.
- The adoption range on the slide (78–88%) is correct for AI Index 2025 → 2026; McKinsey 2026 reportedly puts it at 89% (unverified), i.e. adoption breadth has plateaued while value capture (37% any EBIT, 6% high performers) has not moved.

### Gaps
- McKinsey.com could not be fetched directly (timeouts on page and PDFs). All McKinsey 2025/2026 figures come from search-engine extracts of mckinsey.com pages plus secondary coverage. The 89% / 44% for 2026 and the exact publication day (25 Aug 2026) are secondary only.
- The March 2025 McKinsey edition: sample size, fieldwork dates and any maturity/EBIT figures were not retrieved.
- The wiki thread lists the AI Index 2025 McKinsey sample as n=2,854; this does not match the *Superagency* sample the coordinator gives (≈3,600 employees + 238 C-suite) and was not verified here.
- Whether *State of Organizations 2026*'s "1% mature (US C-suite)" is new data or a re-citation of *Superagency* is unresolved.

## 2. MIT CISR (slide: 7%): is there a 2025–2026 update of the Stage 1–4 distribution?

### Takeaway
Yes. MIT CISR's August 2025 research briefing reports a new distribution: **Stage 4 rose from 7% (2022) to 18% (2025)** and Stage 3 from 31% to 46%. It uses the same scoring rule (Total AI Effectiveness score), but a different and much smaller survey (N=152 vs N=721). The slide's 7% is therefore out of date, and the 18% must be shown with its sample caveat.

### Cited Findings
**Baseline used on the slide**
- Enterprise AI Maturity Model (Weill, Woerner, Sebastian), research briefing 19 Dec 2024, based on the MIT CISR 2022 Future Ready Survey (N=721) plus 9 case studies: Stage 1 28%, Stage 2 34%, Stage 3 31%, **Stage 4 7%** — [MIT CISR, Building Enterprise AI Maturity (Dec 2024)](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2024_1201_EnterpriseAIMaturityModel_WeillWoernerSebastian); [CISR press release 19 Dec 2024 (PDF)](https://cisr.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/EnterpriseAIMaturityModel_CISRpressrelease_121924_0.pdf); popularised in [MIT Sloan Ideas Made to Matter, 7 Oct 2025](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/how-to-boost-your-organizations-ai-maturity-level) (the wiki source page)

**Newer edition: "Grow Enterprise AI Maturity for Bottom-Line Impact", MIT CISR Research Briefing Vol. XXV, No. 8, 21 August 2025 (Woerner, Sebastian, Weill, Káganer)**
- Data: **MIT CISR 2025 Real-Time Business Survey, N=152**; the comparison column uses the 2022 Future Ready Survey (N=721) — [CISR publication page](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2025_0801_EnterpriseAIMaturityUpdate_WoernerSebastianWeillKaganer)
- Stage distribution 2022 → 2025: Stage 1 (Experiment and Prepare) **28% → 13%**; Stage 2 (Build Pilots and Capabilities) **34% → 23%**; Stage 3 (Develop AI Ways of Working) **31% → 46%**; Stage 4 (Become AI Future Ready) **7% → 18%** — [CISR publication page](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2025_0801_EnterpriseAIMaturityUpdate_WoernerSebastianWeillKaganer)
- Measurement: respondents grouped by a "Total AI Effectiveness" score, "the equally weighted combination of three measures" (AI effectiveness at improving operations, improving customer experience, supporting and developing ecosystems). Bands: Stage 1 0–49%, Stage 2 50–74%, Stage 3 75–99%, Stage 4 100% — [CISR publication page](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2025_0801_EnterpriseAIMaturityUpdate_WoernerSebastianWeillKaganer)
- Financial performance vs industry average (growth / profit, percentage points): Stage 1 −26.5 / −15.1; Stage 2 −6.8 / −1.4; Stage 3 +4.7 / +0.8; Stage 4 **+13.9 / +9.9**. The source note says the growth and profit measures are from 2024 — [CISR publication page](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2025_0801_EnterpriseAIMaturityUpdate_WoernerSebastianWeillKaganer)
- Authors' wording: "A new MIT CISR survey has found that enterprises today are making significant progress in their AI maturity… the greatest financial impact is achieved in progressing from stage 2… to stage 3". The survey was "conducted… during a period marked by the widespread adoption of generative AI and experimentation with agentic AI." Four challenges for Stage 2→3: strategy, systems, synchronization, stewardship (cases: Guardian Life, Italgas) — [CISR audio transcript (DOCX), Aug 2025](https://cisr.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2025-08/2025_0801_EnterpriseAIMaturityUpdate_WoernerSebastianWeillKaganer_AudioTranscript.docx)

**2026 follow-ups (no new public distribution)**
- "Scaling Enterprise AI Maturity for Bottom-Line Impact — Session Presentation", Stephanie Woerner, 26 March 2026 (AsiaPac Executive Forum). Members-only; the public page gives no survey or stage figures — [CISR publication page](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2026_0326_ScalingEnterpriseAIMaturity_Woerner)
- Related but different construct: CISR "Leveraging Digital Colleagues for Enterprise Value" (Weill, Woerner, 2026) reports a September 2025 survey of 132 enterprises: 34% actively using digital colleagues, about a third testing, 9% formally integrating (figures from a search summary, not fetched) — [CISR publication page](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2026_0401_DigitalColleagues_WeillWoerner)

### Inferences
- The scoring rule is the same, so the 2025 figure is the "same question", but the sample changed from a 721-firm Future Ready Survey to a 152-firm "Real-Time Business Survey". A real-time CISR survey is likely drawn from the CISR community. That probably skews towards more advanced firms, so part of the 7% → 18% rise may be a sample effect (inference, not stated by CISR).
- Notably, the October 2025 MIT Sloan article that the wiki ingested still quoted the 2022 figures, two months after CISR published the update. This explains why the slide still says 7%.
- With 18%, the CISR number is no longer the second-lowest of the five; it becomes the highest of the "mature share" figures.

### Gaps
- The full briefing PDF (patron portal) was not read; fieldwork month and respondent roles of the N=152 survey are not stated on the public page.
- No 2026 stage distribution is public as of 7 Nov 2026 (the March 2026 presentation is behind a login).

## 3. Werner-Le-Brun (slide: 12%): what is it, and is there any follow-up or newer data?

### Takeaway
"Werner-Le-Brun" is Jana Werner and Phil Le-Brun (both Executives in Residence at AWS), "Become an Octopus Organization", *Harvard Business Review*, Nov–Dec 2025. The 12% ("just 12% of transformation efforts show sustainable performance gains, even after three years") is a general change-management figure, not an AI-maturity measurement. It traces back to McKinsey's 2022 Global Implementation Survey. The only follow-up is the authors' book (HBR Press, March 2026), which brings no new data.

### Cited Findings
- HBR article, Nov–Dec 2025, pp. 50–60, reprint R2506C; key claim "Just 12% of transformations create sustainable performance gains, even after three years", "despite trillions in investment" — [HBR, Become an Octopus Organization](https://hbr.org/2025/11/become-an-octopus-organization) (wiki: `2026-04-28-werner-lebrun-octopus-organization.md`)
- The wiki page labels it "Not primarily an AI article"; the AI angle is implicit (curiosity gap: 73% of executives value curiosity vs 9% of employees who feel leaders support it) — wiki source page, same URL
- Origin of the 12%: McKinsey's 2022 Global Implementation Survey, published in "How to implement transformations for long-term impact": 56% say their programmes met most or all performance goals at least initially, and only 12% report sustaining the gains for more than three years; firms that sustain gains report about twice the financial growth of peers — [McKinsey, How to implement transformations for long-term impact](https://www.mckinsey.com/us/our-insights/how-to-implement-transformations-for-long-term-impact) (search extract; direct fetch timed out). An AHRI interview with Le-Brun also attributes the figure to "oft-cited McKinsey research" — [AHRI](https://www.ahri.com.au/articles/octopus-organisation-thrive-era-of-complexity)
- Book: *The Octopus Organization: A Guide to Thriving in a World of Continuous Transformation*, Harvard Business Review Press, published 25 March 2026 (ISBN 9798892791403) — [AHRI](https://www.ahri.com.au/articles/octopus-organisation-thrive-era-of-complexity); [Publishers Weekly listing](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9798892791403)

### Inferences
- This is a different kind of number from the other four: a transformation-success baseline from 2022 survey data, re-cited in 2025, not a measurement of AI maturity. On a slide about "AI-mature organisations" it should be labelled as a comparison baseline (or dropped).
- Newer data from "the same instrument" would mean a newer McKinsey implementation survey; none was found in this search.

### Gaps
- A secondary summary says the 12% is "of that group" (those who met goals), which would make it 12% of 56%. Other wording ("only 12 percent of respondents") suggests 12% of all respondents. The McKinsey page could not be fetched to settle this. Sample size and fieldwork dates of the 2022 Global Implementation Survey were not retrieved.
- No search was made for a newer McKinsey Global Implementation Survey wave (2024–2026) beyond the query above.

## 4. Cisco / MITTRI (slide: 13%): AI Readiness Index 2025 and 2026, Pacesetter share, and the role of MIT Technology Review Insights

The 13% is Cisco's "Pacesetters" tier (fully ready). It was 14% in 2023, 13% in 2024 and "about 13%" in the 2025 index (14 Oct 2025, third annual edition, >8,000 respondents). MIT Technology Review Insights did **not** run the index: its 2025 report "Building the AI-enabled enterprise of the future" is separate sponsored content, produced with Cisco, that cites the 13%. ### Cited Findings
**Baseline used on the slide**

**Newer edition: Cisco AI Readiness Index 2025, "Realizing the Value of AI" (14 October 2025, third annual)**
- Sample: "over 8,000 AI leaders across 30 markets and 26 industries"; senior IT and business leaders responsible for AI strategy; organisations with 500+ employees; "double-blind"; no research partner named — [Cisco newsroom, 14 Oct 2025](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2025/m10/cisco-ai-research-the-most-ai-ready-companies-outpace-peers-in-the-race-to-value.html)

**2026: CEO study under the same index brand (no new index wave)**
- "How CEOs see AI in 2026": 2,511 CEOs, 23 countries, companies with 250+ employees, data collected January 2026 by Opinion Matters; URL path indicates May 2026. Pacesetters described as "the top 13% of most AI-ready organizations in Cisco's AI Readiness Index"; "Globally, just 13% of organizations are there" — [Cisco, How CEOs see AI in 2026](https://cisco.com/c/m/en_us/solutions/ai/readiness-index/how-ceos-see-ai-in-2026.html)

### Inferences
- The wiki thread's label "Cisco (2025)" and the slide's "Cisco / MITTRI" both point to the 2024 index number re-published in a 2025 MITTRI piece. The best citation is now "Cisco AI Readiness Index 2025 (Oct 2025, n>8,000): ~13% Pacesetters", with the 2024 figure as the earlier value.
- The share is essentially flat (14% → 13% → ~13%) across three waves. Either readiness at the top is not growing, or the tier is defined so that its size stays roughly stable. Cisco does not publish the 2025 scoring method on the pages read, so this cannot be settled.
- The 2025 edition reframes the same tier from "fully ready" to "outperform… across every measure of AI value". The number is comparable as a tier share, but the meaning on the slide ("mature") is Cisco's readiness construct, scored by Cisco's own (vendor) instrument.
- The May 2026 CEO study reuses the 13%; it is not a new measurement of the share.

### Gaps
- The 2025 index's Pacesetter scoring rule, tier shares for Chasers/Followers/Laggards (the landing page rendered them as "0%") and fieldwork dates were not found on the pages read; the 2025 PDF report was not retrieved.
- The exact publication date of the MITTRI–Cisco report was not confirmed (wiki: "2025").

## 5. Ransbotham / Kiron (slide: 15%): MIT SMR–BCG 2025 and 2026 reports, share getting value / "future-built", definitions

### Takeaway
The 15% is the share of **"Augmented Learners"** in the 8th MIT SMR–BCG report (Nov 2024), a 2×2 of organisational learning × AI-specific learning. The 9th report, *The Emerging Agentic Enterprise* (18 Nov 2025, n=2,102), does **not** repeat that segmentation or any "significant value" share. Its headlines are adoption figures (agentic AI 35% + 44% planning; traditional AI 72%). No 10th annual report was found by 7 Nov 2026. BCG's own, separate instruments show a top tier of 5% "future-built" (Sept 2025) and 7.5% (Sept 2026).

### Inferences
- The 15% has no newer comparable figure: the 2025 MIT SMR–BCG report changed its focus from learning capability to agentic adoption and did not re-run the 2×2 classification. The slide should keep "15% (2024)" with its date, or replace it.
- The MIT SMR–BCG adoption series (72% in 2025) is a third adoption measure next to McKinsey's 88%. Both show adoption at a plateau of roughly 70–90%.
- If the slide wants a current "BCG top-tier" number, BCG's own index (5% in 2025 → 7.5% in 2026 future-built) is the nearest, but it is a different instrument (41-capability maturity assessment, CxO sample), not Ransbotham/Kiron.

### Gaps
- The 2026 Applied AI Index's methodology (capabilities scored, fieldwork, whether it is the same instrument as Build for the Future 2025) was not retrieved; comparability of 5% → 7.5% is unconfirmed.
- No share of "extensive agentic adopters" in the 2025 MIT SMR–BCG sample was found in the extracted text.
- Whether a 10th annual MIT SMR–BCG report is scheduled for late 2026 is unknown.
