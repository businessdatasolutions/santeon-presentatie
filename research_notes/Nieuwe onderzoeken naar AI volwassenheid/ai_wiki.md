## Key question 1: What exactly are the five instruments, and what edition is each?

### Takeaway
The thread's own cross-walk lists only **four** instruments (1% / 7% / 12% / 13%). The fifth, Ransbotham/Kiron's 15%, was added later on the concept page `concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.md` and in `syntheses/organizational-frameworks-for-ai-adoption.md`. Two of the five are not first-hand maturity surveys. The 1% is McKinsey data passed on through the AI Index, and the 12% is a general transformation-success figure from an HBR change-management article. The CISR 7% rests on a 2022 survey that predates the GenAI wave.

### Cited Findings
- **AI Index 2025 / McKinsey (1%)**: 8th annual Stanford HAI AI Index, published 2025-04, ingested 2026-04-28. "**Only 1% of C-suite executives** describe their GenAI rollouts as 'mature'": a McKinsey complementary survey of developed-markets executives, via AI Index 2025 §4.4.5. Same edition: "78% of orgs use AI in at least one business function in 2024 (vs. 55% in 2023)", McKinsey survey **n=2,854**; 71% use GenAI. Financial impact: "cost savings <10%, revenue gains <5%". Definition of "mature" = C-suite self-report on GenAI rollouts. [OLDER] — [sources/2026-04-28-ai-index-report-2025.md; concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.md l.214](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report)
- **MIT CISR (7%)**: MIT Sloan "Ideas Made to Matter" article by Kristin Burnham (published 2025-10-07), summarising a CISR briefing by Woerner, Weill, Sebastian and Káganer. Data: "MIT CISR's **2022 Future Ready Survey (N=721)** plus 2024 interviews with 16 executives at 9 enterprises". Distribution: Stage 1 "Experiment and prepare" 28%, Stage 2 "Build pilots and capabilities" 34%, Stage 3 "Develop AI ways of working" 31%, **Stage 4 "Become AI future-ready" 7%**. Maturity score = "equally-weighted combination of three measures: effectiveness of AI to (i) improve operations, (ii) improve customer experience, (iii) support and develop the ecosystem"; Stage 4 = a score of 100%. "Organizations see the greatest financial impact moving from Stage 2 to Stage 3." The page's own caveat: "The 28%/34%/31%/7% stage breakdown should be treated as the *2022 baseline*." [OLDER] — [sources/2026-04-28-mit-sloan-ai-maturity.md](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/how-to-boost-your-organizations-ai-maturity-level)
- **Werner-Le-Brun (12%)**: Jana Werner and Phil Le-Brun (both Executives in Residence at AWS), "Become an Octopus Organization", *Harvard Business Review* Nov–Dec 2025, pp. 50–60, reprint R2506C, adapted from their book *The Octopus Organization*. The page calls it "**Not primarily an AI article**, but a change-management piece". Figure: "**Just 12% of transformation efforts** show sustainable performance gains, even after three years — despite trillions invested in transformations over the past two decades." ### Inferences
- The wiki's "all clustering 7–15%" claim does not hold for the 1% figure, which is an order of magnitude lower. The concept page calls these "seven independent measurements", but only five numbers are listed, and two of them (Werner-Le-Brun, Cisco/MITTRI) are not independent AI-maturity surveys. A slide that says "five instruments" should label the 12% as a general transformation baseline, not an AI-maturity measure.
- Each instrument defines "mature" differently: a self-reported GenAI rollout (1%), a perfect score on an AI-effectiveness index (7%), sustained performance gains from any transformation (12%), self-assessed readiness (13%), and high/high learning capability (15%). The convergence says "the top bar is rare on every scale". It does not say there is one shared maturity rate.

### Gaps
- The wiki does not name the original study behind Werner-Le-Brun's 12%.
- The wiki does not say which Cisco survey produced the 13%. The MITTRI report is a sponsored 8-page piece. Outside the wiki (not verified here): the 13% matches the "Pacesetters" share in Cisco's AI Readiness Index. Check the original before you cite it.
- The wiki gives no sample size or survey name for the McKinsey "1% mature" complementary survey beyond "developed-markets execs". Outside the wiki (not verified here): it is probably McKinsey's "Superagency in the workplace" report (January 2025). Verify before you cite it.

### Inferences

## Key question 4: Do any pages challenge or contradict the "maturity is rare" story?

### Takeaway
No page refutes the story outright, but four lines of evidence qualify it. (1) Under generous definitions, most firms report *some* value, even in the 15% instrument's own data. (2) Adoption breadth is itself instrument-bound: the "78–88%" comes from executive surveys, and official statistics give 10–20%. (3) Firm-level cross-sections may miss maturity that sits with individuals, and they cannot tell a J-curve trough from failure. (4) The wiki's own "convergence" claim is looser than it reads.

### Cited Findings
- **Most firms report some value in the same MIT SMR × BCG survey**: "AI created additional business value over past 3 years": Limited Learners 66%, Organizational Learners 76%, AI-specific 89%, Augmented 95%. "Realized annualized revenue benefits from AI": 71% / 72% / 79% / **99%**. The page flags the 99% as "a near-saturation finding worth flagging". — [sources/2026-05-07-ransbotham-augmented-learners.md](file:///Users/witoldtenhove/Projects/ai-wiki/wiki/sources/2026-05-07-ransbotham-augmented-learners.md)
- **Adoption is instrument-bound**: firm adoption "runs from **~10–20%** (US Census, before and after a wording change, per Hatzius) and **20%** (OECD official statistics) to **69%** (an executive survey in four high-income countries, Yotzov et al.) and **88%** (AI Index)". "The high-adoption, low-maturity gap above may be partly a gap between generous adoption instruments and strict maturity ones." — [threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison.md (section added 2026-11-04)](file:///Users/witoldtenhove/Projects/ai-wiki/wiki/threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison.md). The concept page states it more sharply: "**Any maturity or adoption instrument built on self-reported 'are you using AI' inherits this problem**". — [concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.md l.844](file:///Users/witoldtenhove/Projects/ai-wiki/wiki/concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.md)
- **A missing axis: the worker level, and the trough**: Blum (How I AI, Aug 2026). "If maturity actually accrues to *individuals* first and only later to firms … then firm instruments would systematically under-observe early maturity and over-observe procurement." "A cross-sectional survey cannot distinguish a firm in the trough from a firm whose programme has failed — which may be the single largest source of noise in all four numbers above." Caveat: "N=1, self-reported, unaudited". — [threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison.md (section added 2026-08-31)](file:///Users/witoldtenhove/Projects/ai-wiki/wiki/threads/ai-maturity-measurement-comparison.md)
- **The wiki's own convergence claim**: the concept page says "seven independent measurements all clustering around 7-15% upper-tail" and calls the cohort "structurally narrow, not measurement-artefactual". It lists five numbers, including the 1%. — [concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.md l.100](file:///Users/witoldtenhove/Projects/ai-wiki/wiki/concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption.md)

### Inferences
- The story holds best in this form: "under any strict definition, the mature or value-at-scale share is small (roughly 1–15%), while some use and some value are common". The MIT SMR × BCG appendix shows both halves in one survey, which makes it a useful single-source illustration.
- If the slide keeps "adoption breadth 78–88%", label it as the executive-survey figure. Official firm statistics (OECD 20%, US Census ~10% for use in production) would make the "breadth" bar much shorter. That changes the visual story, though not the conclusion.

### Gaps
- No page measures maturity longitudinally for the same firms, so the "trough vs. failure" question stays open.
- No page validates any of the five maturity instruments against outcomes. The worker-maturity synthesis is explicitly "unvalidated against outcomes".
